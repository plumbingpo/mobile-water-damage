Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Mobile

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Mobile crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. Garage leaks wet whatever is stored on the floor, along with the wall the garage shares with the house. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
What We Do

How We Handle Water Heater Leak Cleanup

1

Remove Standing Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

Soaked insulation, crumbling drywall and damaged baseboards are taken out. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Heater Closet Drywall

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Attic and Ceilings

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.

Items Stored Near the Heater

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Stand and Subfloor Damage

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Flooring type in the neighboring rooms, where the water reached them.
  • Condition of the stand or subfloor below the heater, which may need rebuilding before the replacement is set.
  • Placement of the unit, as leaks from an attic or an inside closet run higher than those in a garage.
  • Quantity of insulation and drywall, on walls or ceilings, that must come out.
