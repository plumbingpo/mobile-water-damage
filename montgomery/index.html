Where Water Damage Starts in Montgomery Properties

Six kinds of calls make up the bulk of our work for Montgomery households. They are all common, and with every one of them waiting adds to the repair.

Mold: Mold is usually the second chapter of a water problem that someone thought was finished. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. When one splits, it sprays until the water is turned off, which can mean hours if nobody is home. The low-cost defense is to swap out aging hoses and learn where the main shutoff is.

Pumps: In homes that have one, a sump pump works unnoticed for years and then quits on the one night it is needed. Stuck float switches, tripped breakers and burned-out motors are what we find most. The result is several inches of water over the lowest floor, soaking stored boxes, flooring and the bottom of the walls. Testing the pump now and then, and adding a battery backup, lowers the odds considerably.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Montgomery, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. In both cases it seeps beneath cabinets and flooring edges, and a moisture meter shows how far it went.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: When storm drains cannot keep up with a downpour, surface water heads for the lowest doorway it can find. Garages, walkout basements and ground-floor rooms at the low side of the lot flood first. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.
