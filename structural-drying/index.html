Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Mobile Restoration Co know it well. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Wall finishes that start to peel or bubble in the days after water damage
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall

Our Structural Drying Process

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Mapping the Moisture

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. Baseline readings come from the map, and so does the plan for placing equipment.

2

Setting the Equipment

Guided by the moisture map, we place high-velocity air movers and commercial LGR dehumidifiers where they will do the most good. We tune airflow and temperature to suit the materials that need drying.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

Our technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment placement and document drying progress. Along the way, we tell you where the drying schedule stands.

4

Dry Standard Verification

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Daily monitoring only works if someone can let the technician into the property
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

The equipment used for professional structural drying is fundamentally different from consumer products. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.
