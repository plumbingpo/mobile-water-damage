Leaking Pipe Water Damage Help in Mobile

Most pipe leaks start without any warning. It drips inside a wall, under a sink or below the slab, and the first thing you notice is a stain, a soft spot in the floor or a water bill that makes no sense. At that point the drywall and wood nearby may have been soaking for weeks. For Mobile homes, our crew maps where the water traveled and dries the hidden damage along with the visible damage.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. This is where our team comes in. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Shut Off the WaterClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. Not sure where it is coming from? Close the main shutoff for the whole house.
  2. Keep Power AwayWater around a switch, outlet or light fixture means the breaker for that part of the house should go off. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Read the MeterMake sure nothing in the house is using water and read the meter. If the dial is still moving, a pipe is leaking somewhere you cannot see, possibly under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographBefore a repair is made or anything goes in the trash, remove what is stored near the leak and get pictures of the pipe, the staining and the warping.
  5. Have the Area MeteredHave the area measured with meters before it is closed up. Inside the cavity, moisture can remain even when the wall surface feels dry.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Leaking Pipe Water Damage

Insurers draw a line between a sudden leak and a slow one. Sudden and accidental damage is often covered, but damage from a leak that went on for weeks or months usually is not, and some policies exclude it by name. A hidden leak you had no way of knowing about may be handled more favorably than one that was visible and ignored. Check your policy and ask your agent. We document the source of the leak, moisture readings and photos so the adjuster has clear facts.

Cost

What Drives the Price

  • Access to the pipe, with a sink cabinet being cheaper to work in than a slab or a closed wall.
  • What the floors and finishes are made of, because certain materials cannot be dried in place.
  • Whether the damage stayed in one room or spread to others and to the level below.
  • Whether the leak lasted days or months, and the size of the area it wet.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Soil movement and rubbing that wear a hole in a line buried below the slab.
  • Tiny pinholes that form as corrosion gradually works through aging copper pipe.
  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
  • Drain fittings and P-traps below sinks that work loose and leak whenever water goes down.
  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Damaged Flooring

Water from a slab or wall leak travels under flooring. The result is cupped hardwood, laminate with swollen seams, and vinyl or tile coming loose as its adhesive fails.

Wet Insulation

Like a sponge, wall insulation takes in water from the pipe and keeps the framing next to it damp. Left in a sealed cavity it can stay wet for a long time, which is why we usually take it out.

Rotted Framing

After weeks of dampness, bottom plates and wall studs start to go soft and rot. Wood that has lost its strength has to be cut out and replaced.

Mold Inside the Wall

Nothing suits mold better than the dark, moist space inside a wall. With a slow leak it often covers the back of the drywall before a single spot shows on the painted side.
